## Start with the decisions the software must support

A platform demo can look convincing before anyone has checked what the proposal includes. A familiar game lobby tells you little about how the operator resolves a disputed transaction, reviews a redemption or moves its data later.

Before comparing providers, write down your player journey and the work your team will do behind it. Include account creation, coin allocation, game sessions, verification, redemption review and staff permissions. Ask each supplier to explain those same workflows. That makes proposals easier to compare than a list of feature names.

## 1. Establish what the delivery model means in the proposal

Treat “turnkey,” “white label” and “custom” as starting points for questions. Do not assume those labels establish identical contract terms across suppliers.

Ask which platform core you will use, what can be configured without development, and what counts as a paid change. Also ask who hosts the platform, who deploys updates and whether your team can use another development partner.

An existing core may suit a product whose requirements fit the available workflows. A more specialized operating model may need deeper development. Compare the proposed scope against [turnkey configuration](/turnkey-sweepstakes-casino-software) and [custom development](/custom-sweepstakes-casino-development), rather than choosing a label first.

## 2. Request a workflow demonstration, not only a lobby tour

Ask the supplier to demonstrate one ordinary journey and one exception. An illustrative test: a player requests a redemption, the review cannot be completed immediately, and an authorized staff member must explain the next step.

Can your team see the request status, relevant records, staff actions and outcome? Are permissions clear? Can an operator investigate without directly changing database records?

For wallet testing, agree examples that show separate coin balances and the effect of a repeated or interrupted transaction. Your technical team should review the expected outcome with the integration documentation. See the [API integration guide](/blog/sweepstakes-casino-api-integration) for the technical questions behind that discussion.

## 3. Separate game availability from permission to use the content

A studio logo on a website does not explain what your contract permits. Request a proposed content list and written confirmation of the commercial and technical arrangements applicable to your product.

Ask who contracts the content, which game modes are supported and what happens if a provider changes availability. Identify whether a new catalog requires configuration, development or another commercial agreement.

Do the same for payment, payout, identity and location services. Record who obtains approval, pays provider charges and supplies production credentials. An integration appearing in a demo is not an approval for your business.

## 4. Compare costs over the same scope

Do not compare one supplier’s setup fee with another supplier’s complete development proposal. Request separate lines for platform delivery, branding, integrations, hosting, maintenance and third-party services.

Where the agreement includes minimum fees, usage charges or revenue share, ask how those charges are calculated. Request an example bill using assumptions you provide. Keep those assumptions consistent across suppliers; they are planning inputs, not traffic or revenue forecasts.

Also ask what triggers a change request. A lower starting price is harder to assess if ordinary workflow changes are excluded and the price of additional work is unspecified.

## 5. Make acceptance testing part of the agreement

Define the scenarios your team must approve before launch. Include successful journeys, rejected requests, provider outages, duplicate notifications and staff access restrictions where relevant to your scope.

Ask which tests happen in a sandbox and what must be rechecked in production. For example, [Sumsub distinguishes sandbox testing from production verification](https://docs.sumsub.com/docs/test-in-sandbox): a successful sandbox exercise should not be presented as proof that real identity checks have passed.

Agree who records defects, who fixes them and what evidence closes a test. Keep any operator legal review and provider approvals separate from technical test completion.

## 6. Read the handover and exit terms before signing

Code access, ownership and operational control are different questions. Confirm what is handed over: repositories, technical documentation, deployment instructions, data exports and administrative access, as applicable to the agreement.

Identify third-party components that have separate licenses. Ask how a later migration would handle account records, balances, transaction history and pending requests. Do not assume migration is included unless the scope says so.

Support also needs a definition. Establish how incidents are reported, which hours are covered and which responsibilities sit with the platform provider, hosting company or another integration partner.

## Questions to bring to the proposal review

### What should a software proposal include?

A useful proposal identifies the delivery model, modules, integrations, responsibilities, exclusions, acceptance criteria and handover terms. It should distinguish one-time delivery work from ongoing and third-party costs.

### Does a turnkey platform include every integration?

Not necessarily. Ask for an integration list showing what already exists, what needs configuration, what requires development and which services depend on separate commercial approval.

### Will custom development give me full source-code ownership?

That depends on the agreement. Ask which code transfers, which components remain licensed and what access you receive. Confirm the terms before treating ownership as a reason to choose a supplier.

### How can I compare launch timelines?

Compare the milestones and dependencies behind the dates. Branding, integration work, provider onboarding, testing and operational approvals may have different owners. A date without those assumptions is difficult to evaluate.

### What should I send when requesting a quote?

Share your intended operating markets, delivery preference, required workflows, existing systems, provider preferences, budget range and target date. Mark assumptions that still need review so the team can distinguish requirements from unresolved decisions.
